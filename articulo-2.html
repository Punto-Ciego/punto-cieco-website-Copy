The Cost of Copper: Illegal Mining in Peru

The global demand for copper can carry local environmental and social costs.
01 · Anthropology

How does it reshape communities?

Illegal mining can bring income to rural communities, but it can also weaken local protections and create serious social and environmental problems.

Some operations are linked to criminal groups, unsafe work, unregulated waste and abuses against residents. Even when mining is run by local people, that does not automatically mean workers’ rights or environmental rules are protected.

Copper also passes through processing plants before it reaches buyers. That long supply chain can make it harder to trace where the mineral came from.

Mining can change livelihoods, local power and working conditions.
02 · Economy

Why is illegal mining profitable?

Copper is used in phones, cars and power lines. Global demand can make it valuable, creating an incentive for illegal operators to sell it.

Illegal businesses may also avoid taxes and environmental costs that legal companies must pay.

High demand, avoided costs and weak oversight can make illegal mining attractive to criminal groups.

When operators ignore safety standards or use exploitative labour, their costs can fall further—at the expense of workers and communities.

Tracing copper through complex supply chains is important because illegally sourced minerals can be mixed with legally produced material.

03 · Government

Why is enforcement so difficult?

Peru faces a difficult balance: mining is economically important, but illegal operations can damage ecosystems and undermine state authority.

Government responses include inspections, seizing equipment, environmental coordination and efforts to move informal operators into legal activity.

Enforcement remains challenging when records can be falsified and minerals move through several stages before reaching buyers. Effective oversight requires cooperation across agencies and the supply chain.

Monitoring extraction and transport is part of enforcement.
04 · History

What role will copper play in Peru’s future?

Copper has long supported Peru’s economy, and demand is expected to remain important as countries expand electrification and renewable-energy systems.

But economic benefits do not erase the costs of illegal extraction. Mining can threaten biodiversity and Indigenous communities, especially where environmental safeguards and protections for local residents are weak.

The challenge is to support lawful production while improving oversight, protecting communities and reducing environmental harm.

05 · Youth opinion

What should come first?

In our Instagram poll, readers had different views on the balance between stopping illegal mining and supporting copper production.

Some prioritized tackling illegal mining; others prioritized economic growth. A third view supported both: reducing illegal activity while strengthening legal copper production.
