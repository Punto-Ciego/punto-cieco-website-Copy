Why Do Less Diverse Countries Profit More from Natural Resources Than Diverse Countries?

Natural resources, economic development, and who benefits from them.
01 · Anthropology

Does diversity affect who benefits from natural resources?

Diversity seems like nothing but benefits on paper. It should create economic growth, with the potential of eco-tourism and sustainable resource use , yet it seems that economic power and biodiversity are mutually exclusive. Most economic growth, especially when regarding production, comes from the extreme exploitation of natural resources, causing this biodiversity to slowly die.

A great example of the situation is shown in Nigeria, a country rich in natural resources, mainly oil, that while heavily exploited, has shown little-to-no development, with those getting richer being the western powers like USA and multiple EU members. This is oftentimes called the “resources curse”, explaining why resource rich countries especially in South America and Africa, lack in development as more powerful countries exert control through modern forms of imperialism.

Experts on the World Bank report also note that the heavy exploitation of natural resources creates a further division among the classes, with this being especially profitable for those in positions of power while disregarding the smaller businesses and communities that depend on local ecosystems.

02 · Economy

Why does the US capture more value from resources than Peru?

One of the best raw materials in Peru is copper. This material helps Peru make a lot of money. Other countries, like the US, don’t have the same advantages as Peru in terms of natural resources, but they have good organization and more developed supply chains. It seems that Peru doesn’t have the same level of development in these areas.

In the case of the US, it has invested a lot of money in industries, infrastructure, plants, etc. All of this has the goal of creating more value and making more money. In contrast, the problem in Peru is that it doesn’t invest enough in its supply chains, industries, and infrastructure like the US and other countries do. This makes it more difficult for Peru to create more value from its resources.

Another important thing is education. Many countries invest in better education to prepare people who can develop these supply chains and work in these industries. In conclusion, if Peru invests more in education, it will have more skilled workers and can improve its supply chains and create more value from its resources.

03 · Government

Do governments determine who profits from natural resources?

Illegal copper mining is a growing and very profitable business today. Why? The main reason is the rising global demand for this metal. Copper is needed everywhere to build cars, phones, and power lines.

Because the global demand is so high, the price of copper remains high. Criminal groups take advantage of this high price to make fast money. But how are they able to keep the profits?

First, they avoid paying taxes or following environmental rules. Formal mining companies pay millions of dollars to the government, but illegal miners keep all the profits for themselves. Furthermore, they often use cheap, forced labor and ignore safety laws.

As experts report, this stolen metal is secretly sold in international markets, making the black market extremely attractive. In short, high prices, zero taxes, and low costs make it a very dark but lucrative trade.

04 · History

How did history shape Peru’s and the US’s resource economies?

Peru stands out as one of the countries with the world’s largest copper reserves and is already a one of the world’s leading copper producers and with the promising future of the global energy transition, Peru will be a central player in guaranteeing this mineral for its properfunctioning. In the past, copper extraction boosted the national economy, accounting for nearly 23% of the country’s total exports and the international copper prices increased by more than 70%, strengthening mining investment. This sector is a cornerstone of Peru’s economy, yet, since the early 2000s, the illegal mining, centered on gold, coal and copper, operates in over half the country and the high demand has only boosted the expansion of this damaging activity, leading to biodiversity loss in indigenous and native community territories due to the lack of environmental defenders. The future of our country depends on achieving a satisfactory balance between state governance and economic development.

05 · Young perspectives

Opinion

We did an Instagram poll to ask our readers’ opinions about why countries with less natural resource diversity often profit more from their resources. We received many interesting and diverse opinions. The majority of people believe that it is not possible for two reasons: we don’t have the amount of resources required and people believe that we will not be able to surpass the USA because it has one of the biggest industries in the world.

However, others think that we do have what is necessary: if we properly take advantage of our rich diversity, we will surely achieve it.
